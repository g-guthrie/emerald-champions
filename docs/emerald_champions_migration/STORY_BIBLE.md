# Emerald Champions story bible

## Promise

Emerald Champions is still the Hoenn campaign: Team Magma and Team Aqua force
the land and sea out of balance, Rayquaza ends the catastrophe, Wallace tests
the player as Champion, and the Battle Frontier is the true endless endgame.

The rebuilt story makes the region feel newly alive without replacing that
spine. Ancient **Champion's Signs** have begun shining across Hoenn. They react
to prepared teams, trusted partners, Mega Stones, and the weather disturbance
caused by Magma and Aqua. The player is not collecting arbitrary quest tokens;
they are proving that they can answer every kind of battle and restoring the
network that once kept Groudon, Kyogre, and Rayquaza in balance.

## Narrative rules

1. Every new area must advance at least two of exploration, team access,
   progression rewards, legendary discovery, or the central story.
2. Information lives in native NPC dialogue, inscriptions, scenery, and the
   PokéNav—not floating quest markers or a new quest UI.
3. Every legendary challenge states a readable requirement. Riddles can be
   elegant, but the player must understand that a Champion's Sign and a rare
   encounter are involved.
4. Magma seeks stable, controllable land energy. Aqua seeks stable,
   controllable sea energy. Both gradually discover that Primal power cannot
   be controlled and that their machines are awakening every Sign in Hoenn.
5. Steven and Devon study the physical side—stones, caves, and energy. The Mt.
   Pyre wardens preserve the spiritual history. Wallace understands that the
   real answer is balance. None of them replaces the player's discovery.
6. Rayquaza remains the campaign climax. Legendary side quests enrich that
   climax; they never make another Pokémon the secret "real" savior.
7. The Battle Frontier is the final expression of the same thesis: unlimited
   competitive puzzles after the authored regional examination is complete.

## Campaign acts

### Act I — A region that takes battling seriously

- The rival asks which regional starter tradition the player trusts.
- Birch frames the Pokédex as field research, while the Pokémon Center tools
  make team preparation immediate.
- Roxanne is the first true Champion examination, not a tutorial boss.
- The Devon president asks the player to watch for geometric marks appearing
  in caves and forests after the first Magma/Aqua theft.
- Expanded Petalburg Woods introduces the phrase **Champion's Sign** and the
  first readable partner-gated encounters: Virizion and Celebi.

### Act II — Stones, trust, and the first fault line

- Brawly tests commitment and redirection.
- Steven gives the Mega Ring after Brawly and explains that a stone is only a
  key; the bond and tactical decision supply the energy.
- Dewford Meadow and the abandoned manor reveal Hoenn's earliest Mega research
  and point toward a crystal chamber under Sootopolis.
- Slateport Archie now battles the player after both grunts, making Aqua's
  competence and ideology concrete rather than purely expositional.
- Wattson and the expanded electric Gym establish that each specialty can
  contain many different competitive doctrines.

### Act III — The region cracks open

- Meteor Falls includes a real Magma formation battle before Aqua interrupts.
- Mt. Chimney's machine destabilizes Ember Path, Ashen Woods, Sandstrewn Ruins,
  Scorched Slab, and Seaspray Cave.
- Flannery tests whether the player can manage the same heat Magma is trying to
  weaponize.
- Ashen Woods shows the constructive side of land and fire: destruction,
  regrowth, weather, and four distinct battle schools.
- Moltres and the Mega Stone in Ember Path reward Strength-based exploration.

### Act IV — Balance is a practice

- Norman is the personal midpoint: power without adaptation is not mastery.
- Verdanturf Meadow and Shaymin show recovery after environmental damage.
- The Weather Institute identifies the widening cave network and explains why
  Castform can awaken Landorus at the desert summit.
- Fortree and Winona examine speed, positioning, and the sky without repeating
  one Tailwind puzzle.
- Sandstrewn Ruins preserves every fossil as a record of a different survival
  answer; Landorus and Zygarde form its surface/depth legendary chain.

### Act V — Land and sea lose control

- Aqua's activity exposes Seaspray Cave. Its lower chamber bends space around
  Palkia's Sign, gated by Kingdra and the postgame story state.
- Magma's activity opens Scorched Slab. Heatran guards the earth; Reshiram's
  Sign waits deeper for the completed dream/time chain.
- Mt. Pyre explicitly connects the old orbs, the Champion's Signs, and
  Rayquaza's role as mediator. Darkrai, Cresselia, and Dialga remain the linked
  nightmare/dream/time side story.
- Mossdeep ties the celestial disturbances to Jirachi and makes Courtney's
  final formation part of the Space Center conflict.

### Act VI — The sky answers

- Cave of Origin exposes the Diancie chamber and closes the Mega-research clue.
- Groudon and Kyogre are still the disaster Magma and Aqua created.
- Sky Pillar and Rayquaza remain the only immediate solution: the player has
  learned throughout the region that victory means restoring the board, not
  simply adding a stronger attacker.
- Wallace's Champion battle is the final authored regional examination.

### Postgame — Distortion and endless mastery

- Altering Cave becomes Hoopa's distortion sanctuary and Leaf's one-time
  superboss examination.
- Meteor Falls' hidden Jirachi chamber and the remaining postgame Signs reward
  complete exploration.
- The Legendary Sign mastery path ends with Arceus only after all finite
  acquisition methods are complete.
- The live Showdown-derived Champions Circuit and native Battle Frontier are
  the true endless endgame. They generate new competitive problems rather than
  recycling campaign rematches.

## Dialogue standard

- Ordinary trainers speak about the concrete idea their team demonstrates,
  but in short native lines rather than strategy-guide monologues.
- Gym trainers remain faithful to the leader's type while presenting different
  tactical interpretations.
- Boss introductions establish motive and battle identity; defeat lines reveal
  character rather than merely expressing surprise.
- NPC clues name the relevant location or Pokémon family often enough that a
  player can solve the quest without an external guide.
- All final strings must pass GBA width and pagination checks.
