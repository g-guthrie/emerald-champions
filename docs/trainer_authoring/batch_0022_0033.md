# Individual trainer authoring: E0022–E0033

Design review, September 8, 2026. E0022 has no authored branch. The other assigned encounters contain **19 branches / 84 Pokémon**, including both owners of paired encounters and all six Rustboro rival aliases. Only these canonical blocks were edited; generated trainers and runtime tables must be materialized by the integration owner.

## Evidence and interpretation

The authority for moves is the current `src/data/pokemon/emerald_champions_preparation_learnsets.h` All Legal Moves arrays, not imported audit learnsets or evolution-family assumptions. All 336 selected move slots pass membership in those arrays; all 84 abilities pass the configured, preprocessed species table. In particular, Wooloo does not have Body Press, Nacli does not have Salt Cure, and Marshtomp does not have High Horsepower. Those draft ideas were rejected, not added to player access. Conversely, this game's configured Zubat does have Tailwind and Finneon/Skrelp do have Scald.

Speed values below are derived from current `CalculateMonStatsCont` in `src/pokemon.c`: forced 31 IVs, flat Stat Points added before nature, integer floors. They are not conventional EV calculations. The normal-difficulty deployment level follows `ApplyTrainerLevelDifficulty` in `src/difficulty.c`: `max(authored level, live badge cap) - 2`, floored at 1. Thus negative offsets before Roxanne are level 12, not levels 10–11. Positive authored offsets can remain above the live floor. `GetCurrentLevelCap` in `src/caps.c` gives 14 before the Stone Badge and 20 after it; the Rusturf grunt's authored 20/21 already exceeds the pre-badge floor. Ned's negative-offset fish become level 18 alongside the others because the live cap is 20. Hard/Easy shift those Normal values by +2/−2; returning after later badges can change thresholds again.

Each spread totals 66 points; a main investment is 32 points. PS/SS invest the attacking category and Speed, PB/SB invest HP and the attacking category, WD/WS invest HP and the corresponding defense, and FS invests HP/Speed. Zero Speed points is not a zero IV. Every listed item and move is considered with its actual role; Eviolite is not an automatic reason to remove offensive investment at these flat-stat low levels.

Current native battle repair evidence covers the shared Storm Drain, partner-damage, redirection, speed-control, Trick Room and support machinery. **It does not playtest these 19 newly authored rosters.** The individual scores below judge design toward the stated intention, not measured win rate or an exhaustive simulation. Counterplay is available through current preparation and the actual early habitat roster: Route101 Mienfoo, Route102 Timburr/Pachirisu, Route116 Fighting options and ordinary Grass/Water/Electric coverage are not replaced by a required owned starter. Availability does not assert that every suggested move is learned naturally at level 12.

Species distribution was counted across canonical branches, not unique playthrough encounters. This batch introduces the first Sandile and Minccino occurrences and removes the earlier duplicate Cranidos. Archen remains twice, with different supporting boards; Lileep/Tirtouga, Shieldon and Nacli each retain a single occurrence. Six mutually exclusive rival aliases inflate Taillow/Shroomish/Electrike totals and must not be mistaken for six consecutive encounters. A second Skrelp near Rusturf is retained as a regional special-attacking connection, not copied as another rain lead.

## E0023 — TRAINER_CLARK — 8.5/10

Historical source-only review below. The executable-AI restart is now complete
for Clark alone; see [ai_restart_0023_clark.md](ai_restart_0023_clark.md) for
native outcomes, repaired forecasts, accepted Protect/pressure changes and
current limitations. Clark and Johnson actually start separate doubles battles.

Intent: a physical-defense lesson with an immediate attacking partner, then a fast finish. Roggenrola keeps Eviolite/Sturdy and 32 Defense for actual Iron Defense/Body Press payoff; Wide Guard replaces low-immediacy hazards. Rock Tomb Geodude attacks or changes one target's order. Its 45 Speed precedes Roggenrola's 12 outside Room; Mudbray is 19 and Archen 62, all level 12. Rock Head removes Double-Edge recoil but not Life Orb recoil. Mudbray's HP/Attack investment gives Stamina something worth preserving, while itemless Archen keeps doubled Acrobatics power.

Transition: use Mudbray when physical contact is survivable; use Archen to finish or pressure a Grass/Fighting answer. Neither reserve needs Roggenrola to have boosted first. Single-target Water/Grass/Fighting bypasses Wide Guard; special damage avoids Stamina, and chip below half HP constrains Defeatist. Current early Fighting access makes this an actual preparation choice. Archen repeats later at Marc intentionally as a recognizable threshold mechanic. Improvement below 10: playtest whether the lead's conditional defensive turns delay the encounter unnecessarily against special teams; no assertion that Iron Defense is always optimal.

## E0023 — TRAINER_JOHNSON — 8.4/10

Historical source-only design below, including obsolete stats and Return prose.
The [final Gen9 native review](e0023_johnson_gen9_2026_09_09.md) supersedes this
section's team details, tactical assertions and rating.

Intent: Intimidate creates one attacking window for a recoil bird, followed by independent Huge Power or Guts offense. Shinx retains Eviolite/HP/Attack. Sharp Beak replaces Starly's Life Orb to avoid paying two recoil sources. Bunnelby gains real Return STAB and legal Brick Break instead of redundant coverage/pivoting. Its 54 Speed and 32 Attack points support immediate damage; priority Quick Attack remains useful. At level 12 the others are Shinx 19, Starly 60 and Taillow 67.

Transition: Bunnelby breaks Rock/Steel or screens; Taillow Protects for Toxic Orb only when the resulting Guts window exceeds the lost attack. All four are physical, deliberately creating useful Intimidate/burn counterplay rather than pretending there is an unanswerable line. Special attacks ignore the opening Intimidate; Ghosts resist Return but not Shinx's Crunch or every bird option. This preserves the regional early birds while avoiding another identical Life Orb set. Improvement: measure Toxic Orb's multi-turn cost against Flame Orb in actual four-member fights; the current choice retains a short-stay Guts identity without claiming an optimal battle length.

## E0024 — TRAINER_DEVAN — 9.0/10

Historical source-only design below. The [final Gen9 native review](e0024_gen9_2026_09_09.md)
supersedes its flat-stat speeds, Smooth Rock/hazard set and provisional rating.

Intent: automatic sand provides immediate order advantage, with a distinct Moxie cleanup reserve. Hippopotas keeps Smooth Rock, HP/Defense, Yawn and recovery. Drilbur changes Jolly to Adamant: at level 12 its 57 Speed becomes 114 in sand, so the nature can improve damage instead of buying redundant speed against most early unboosted foes. Sandile replaces the second Scarf Cranidos and uses Eviolite, Jolly PS and legal Stomping Tantrum/Crunch. Aron remains a Sturdy Steel answer, not a second weather setter.

Transition: preserve Hippopotas if weather replacement matters; introduce Sandile at 61 Speed when a realistic KO can start Moxie, not blindly into Fighting/Fairy. Other speeds are Hippopotas 16 and Aron 15. Sand immunity keeps every reserve compatible. Grass/Water and weather replacement remain strong answers; Sturdy still demands chip or coordinated attacks. Sandile's first campaign appearance improves species distribution and leaves Marc's Scarf fossil distinctive. Improvement: native checks should compare weather denial and fast priority teams rather than merely proving doubled Speed on an empty field.

## E0025 — TRAINER_DAWSON — 8.7/10

Intent: Fake Out enables a useful Tailwind, then two different Normal reserves exploit positioning. Meowth retains Tough Claws/Eviolite and HP/Attack. Rookidee retains Sash and Speed investment, with Taunt as an alternative to Tailwind. Wooloo is deliberately **not** given nonexistent Body Press: Eviolite/Fluffy and HP/Attack turn it into a contact-resistant immediate attacker with Thunder Wave. Minccino replaces Tandemaus; Skill Link makes a successful Tail Slap hit five times, Silk Scarf boosts its real STAB, and Encore answers a demonstrated passive turn. Tail Slap can still miss.

Level-12 speeds are Meowth 18, Rookidee 59, Wooloo 20 and Minccino 63; Tailwind does not magically make Wooloo the fastest Pokémon. U-turn can bring the appropriate reserve, while Minccino's own pivot avoids a bad contact matchup. Non-contact Fire exploits Fluffy, special damage bypasses contact reduction, and Ghost/contact-punishment answers Minccino remain relevant despite Knock Off. The first Minccino gives this route a distinct multihit specialist. Improvement: verify Tailwind is chosen only at useful crossings and measure whether Wooloo's recoil consumes too much of its defensive niche.

## E0025 — TRAINER_SARAH — 8.8/10

Intent: anti-Taunt Room support enables special pressure and slow reserves. Spritzee changes to Relaxed WD and gains Psychic instead of a second Fairy attack; Aroma Veil genuinely protects against ordinary Taunt. Gothita retains Sash/Quiet SB. Fidough gains Eviolite and priority Helping Hand instead of Take Down, so the slower partner plan does not depend on unsupported physical damage. Baltoy retains Levitate and special Ground coverage.

All are level 12: Spritzee 12, Gothita 17, Fidough 21 and Baltoy 18. These are slow relative to heavily Speed-invested early teams, not zero-IV minima. Fake Tears is conditional on a later beneficiary; it is not guaranteed to precede Gothita's own attack. Remove Spritzee, suppress Aroma Veil, use a genuinely slower attacker, or stall finite Room. Steel/Poison attacks must consider Psychic/Ground retaliation. Fairy/Psychic repetition serves a single local plan with Baltoy changing immunity and coverage. Improvement: native board tests should check mixed opposing speeds and whether a lone surviving Fidough sensibly abandons support.

## E0026 — TRAINER_JANICE — 8.8/10

Intent: protect Huge Power offense from redirectable Electric attacks while retaining special and priority alternatives. Pichu keeps Sash, Lightning Rod, fast Fake Out/Nuzzle and invested Thunderbolt. Marill replaces redundant Body Slam with Knock Off; Eviolite and HP/Attack retain its priority Aqua Jet and Fairy pressure. Swirlix's Sitrus/Unburden is a conditional speed mode, not an automatic first-turn boost. Chewtle remains a Strong Jaw breaker rather than another setup task.

At level 12: Pichu 60, Marill 18, Swirlix 52 (104 after actual consumption), Chewtle 51. Ground threatens Pichu without donating Lightning Rod boosts; Grass pressures Marill. A double target can break Sash and finish Pichu, while special Swirlix stops physical mitigation from answering every member. This retains useful babies and distinguishes Swirlix from another orbital-status physical finisher. Improvement: check Sitrus consumption timing and useful coverage choices against realistic early teams; neither Unburden nor Lightning Rod guarantees survival.

## E0026 — TRAINER_JERRY_1 — 8.5/10

Intent: a supported Steel lead transitions into Poison/Water special offense and a physical priority reserve. Magnemite retains Sturdy/Eviolite and Speed/special points; Balloon Varoom avoids the immediate shared Ground hit and uses Taunt or physical damage. Skrelp replaces non-STAB Dragon Pulse with legal Scald while retaining Hydro Pump for higher damage; Adaptability applies to Water/Poison, not Thunderbolt. Grimer preserves item removal and Shadow Sneak with HP/Attack and Black Sludge.

Level-12 speeds are 51/20/15/14 in roster order. Magnemite can pivot into a better recipient; Skrelp must respect its actual Specs lock. Break Balloon before Ground damage, pressure Magnemite's Sturdy appropriately, or exploit Skrelp's chosen move. Water typing prevents a Fire-only answer to the entire party. Keeping one early Skrelp special cannon is distinct from the later E31 Eviolite/Acid Spray role. Improvement: evaluate whether Varoom's slow Taunt is worth its slot on common attacking boards; it is retained for a real anti-support option, not required every turn.

## E0027 — TRAINER_JOSH — 8.2/10

Intent: the first gym trainer teaches Sturdy, spread protection and targeted support without relying on an unsupported double-Counter prediction game. Bonsly changes defensive points to HP/Attack and uses legal Stomping Tantrum/Helping Hand. Shieldon retains special bulk and offers legal Wide Guard, Rock Tomb and Taunt. Rolycoly replaces low-payoff Heat Crash with Will-O-Wisp; Nacli uses ordinary Rock damage and recovery, not unavailable Salt Cure. Dwebble's Hard Stone replaces redundant Sash on Sturdy. Alolan Geodude retains its Electric coverage and Lum protection.

All are level 12; speeds are 11/13/15/14/53/13. Helping Hand's priority can support any actual selected attack despite Bonsly's slowness. Dwebble is an intentional faster phase, not another defensive rock. Special Water/Grass, single-target Fighting, multihit attacks and chip provide distinct ways through; burns do not stop special attacks. All six preserve Rock gym identity and rare early forms. Improvement: this is the most defense-heavy branch, so actual turn counts and useful Taunt/burn choices need scrutiny. A six-member slog would merit further role compression, not a fake forced-support bonus.

## E0028 — TRAINER_TOMMY — 9.2/10

Intent: a real, conditional Storm Drain partner engine inside the Rock gym. Reordered Tirtouga into the lead beside Lileep; Tirtouga is now Modest SS with Mystic Water and Surf, Lileep Modest SB/Eviolite. Level-12 Tirtouga's 46 Speed precedes Lileep's 14, so a successful Surf immunity activation can raise Special Attack before Lileep acts. Authored `ACTIVATE TIRTOUGA SURF LILEEP` makes that species partnership available to reserve planning without forcing friendly fire.

Anorith becomes Jolly, 63 Speed, and remains the physical reserve; Shuckle 9 offers conditional Helping Hand/Encore, Nosepass 13 changes coverage, Binacle 52 supplies Tough Claws offense. Surf is unsafe beside a nonimmune reserve unless the actual resulting action pair makes it safe. Fighting pressures the lead, Grass pressures Tirtouga, and Lileep is only neutral to Grass; remove one actor or use appropriate immunity/coverage. This keeps unique fossil occurrences and adds a distinct synergy rather than another weather team. Improvement: run this exact team against Water-immune opponents and a forced partner change; generic Connie Storm Drain evidence is not this roster's runtime certification.

## E0029 — TRAINER_MARC — 9.0/10

Intent: credible Veil establishment protects a mixed-speed offensive Rock team. Amaura changes from slow offensive Light Clay to Timid FS/Focus Sash: level 12 Speed 56 buys meaningful order and a survival window, at the cost of only five Veil turns. Icy Wind adds conditional speed control. Power Spot Stonjourner keeps immediate physical pressure and Wide Guard. Tyrunt gains Dragon Claw rather than another Rock attack. Klawf, itemless Archen and Scarf Cranidos keep different late modes.

Other Normal values: Stonjourner level 13/27 Speed, Tyrunt 13/58, Klawf 13/28, Archen 14/66, Cranidos 13/56 (84 with Scarf). Deny snow before casting, double-target Sash, remove Veil with a valid tool, attack Stonjourner specially, or exploit Choice lock/Defeatist. Ending snow does not erase established Veil. Cranidos is now the sole authored occurrence; Archen repeats as an identifiable but differently supported finisher. Improvement: native test that Veil is worthwhile against immediate double-target pressure and that Icy Wind does not steal a winning attack.

## E0030 — TRAINER_ROXANNE_1 — 9.1/10

Intent: preserve Roxanne's deliberate slow Rock identity while making iconic Nosepass a real damage/support phase. Carbink/Mental Herb/Sturdy, Band Relicanth, Guts Larvitar, Rock Head Sudowoodo and the final Bombirdier remain because their pair roles already differ. Nosepass changes from Sassy special-defense investment to Quiet HP/special attack; Eviolite/Sturdy retains durability, while Power Gem/Earth Power no longer rely only on low base offense. Wide Guard is conditional protection, not mandatory repetition.

Normal level/Speed: Carbink 14/20, Relicanth 15/23, Larvitar 15/18, Sudowoodo 16/17, Nosepass 16/17, Bombirdier 18/36. Carbink is the only Room setter; Light Screen is optional, and Bombirdier may prefer ordinary late order. Mental Herb absorbs one applicable disruption, not all denial. Prepared slow teams, repeated denial, coordinated attacks, spread-versus-single-target selection and multiple coverage types remain counterplay. Nosepass retains the leader's identity instead of replacing it with a generic stronger Rock. Improvement: exact first-gym tests must demonstrate both the Room line and a credible ordinary early-party answer; no claim of those wins is made here.

## E0031 — TRAINER_GRUNT_RUSTURF_TUNNEL — 8.9/10

Intent: double Intimidate into coordinated physical pressure, then a special or fast support reserve. Keep Mightyena/Qwilfish's Howl, paralysis and distinct STAB; replace Zubat's Confuse Ray with the game's legal Tailwind. Native Inner Focus also resists Intimidate. Skrelp retains Eviolite and Acid Spray rather than repeating Jerry's Specs role.

Normal level/Speed is Mightyena 19/75, Qwilfish 19/43, Skrelp 18/21 and Zubat 19/69. These levels follow authored 20/21 minus two even if encountered before Roxanne; they are not inferred solely from the later live cap. Tailwind is useful only for actual order changes; Howl needs a worthwhile physical partner. Special damage, Defiant, suitable ability/item preparation, Ground/Electric coverage and careful Psychic positioning can dismantle the opening. Zubat counters a careless Fighting-only solution. Improvement: native tests should check two Intimidates versus an anti-drop lead and preserve the special-reserve transition rather than repeatedly attempting Howl.

## E0032 — rival runtime boundary

`CreateNPCTrainerParty` calls `ApplyRegionalRivalStarter` in `src/battle_setup.c`. The selected unchosen regional starter can replace the Hoenn starter slot, then `ApplyEmeraldChampionsRegionalRivalSet` applies that species' native preset, point allocation and item-collision handling. The other three members persist. If the species remains the authored Hoenn species, these exact authored sets remain. Therefore each alias below is reviewed individually, but **the dynamic regional closer variants still require separate native generation/interaction verification**. They are not silently counted as tested by the six catalogue branches.

## E0032 — TRAINER_BRENDAN_RUSTBORO_MUDKIP — 8.8/10

Guts Taillow/Lightning Rod Electrike remains a coherent physical/special lead; Effect Spore Eviolite Shroomish is a real Spore/recovery reserve, not the previously claimed Poison Heal Breloom. Grovyle trades X-Scissor/Quick Attack redundancy for Drain Punch/Rock Slide, giving the Grass closer meaningful Steel/Fire/Flying coverage. Normal speeds are Taillow level 19/82, Electrike 19/73, Shroomish 19/24 and Grovyle 20/89; Grovyle retains Jolly PS/Life Orb because it is a genuinely fast physical closer. Drain Punch can recover HP but is not free Life Orb immunity.

Ground removes Electric support; physical mitigation, anti-powder positioning and focused damage into the frail closer remain answers. The common three-member backbone deliberately establishes rival identity; this alias is mutually exclusive with the others. Improvement: verify the actual Grass regional replacement portfolio, not just Grovyle, and test a bird-lost reserve board before claiming all variants equally strong.

## E0032 — TRAINER_BRENDAN_RUSTBORO_TORCHIC — 8.7/10

Retain the same supported Taillow/Electrike lead and Shroomish reserve. Marshtomp gains legal Earthquake instead of Rock Slide, with Waterfall/Ice Punch/Protect retained. Its Normal level 20/31 Speed and Sitrus HP/Attack create a bulky alternative to the fast Grass/Fire closers. Earthquake's safe partner is Flying Taillow; it is not permission to hit Electrike or Shroomish. Their speeds remain 82/73/24 at level 19.

Grass threatens Marshtomp fourfold and ignores Spore, but Brave Bird prevents a blind Grass-only answer. Intimidate/burn can constrain the two physical attackers. Species repetition deliberately preserves parity and rival recognition. Improvement: exact native partner-change evidence should prove Waterfall/Ice Punch replaces dangerous Earthquake when grounded allies remain; regional Water replacements need separate review.

## E0032 — TRAINER_BRENDAN_RUSTBORO_TREECKO — 8.8/10

## E0033 — TRAINER_NED — 9.0/10

Intent: a manual rain setter whose native speed actually precedes its partner, with useful special and support pivots. Finneon keeps Timid SS/Damp Rock and replaces Hydro Pump with legal Scald for a reliable non-setup attack. At the first reachable live cap 20, all four are Normal level 18: Finneon 72, Arrokuda 66, Mantyke 28 and Tentacool 35. Rain therefore gives Finneon/Arrokuda 144/132, and can affect Arrokuda's same-turn order. The old claim based on authored levels 19/20 was rejected after tracing the live floor.

Life Orb/Adamant PS Arrokuda retains immediate physical power; Finneon U-turn or Arrokuda Flip Turn can bring Water Absorb Mantyke's Helping Hand or Tentacool's Acid Spray/Icy Wind when the recipient is genuinely better. Electric threatens the Water roster; Grass threatens the lead but must respect Ice coverage and Tentacool's Poison attack. Water immunity and weather replacement force alternate lines. This retains Finneon as a distinctive setter rather than replacing it with another automatic rain species. Improvement: test setter denial and safe pivot choices under rain; speed arithmetic does not independently prove the best move on a populated board.
