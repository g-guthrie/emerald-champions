September 8, 2026. Eleven branches; E0016 is not an existing encounter. All are
four-Pokémon doubles at the initial cap of 14. Party sizes, world placement and

## Actual game rules and evidence

The editable input is `data/emerald_champions/emerald_champions_battle_teams.txt`.
`emerald_champions_teams.py` feeds the master and compiled plan table;
`implement_emerald_champions_master_battles.py` feeds trainers.party, then
trainerproc feeds the native trainer table. `CreateNPCTrainerPartyFromTrainer`
and `GenerateMonFromTrainerMon` consume that table. None of these eleven is a
regional-rival substitution branch. First two authored slots are the intended
lead; ordinary switch/replacement choices remain conditional. Winston also
retains the last-slot Ace Pokemon instruction.

`src/difficulty.c` applies the live cap floor before Easy -4, Normal -2 or Hard
0. At the initial cap this gives level 10/12/14 for all nonpositive offsets;
Grimer's +1 gives 11/13/15. Return visits use the later live cap. Stat Points are
**flat additions before Nature**, not EV/4. Derived unmodified Speed values below
use the configured species table and `src/pokemon.c`'s native formula. Speed
control, priority, items and field effects are applied separately.

After the static-check audit, all **176 authored move slots** were checked by
executing the production `CanSpeciesUseEmeraldChampionsPreparationMove` function
against the real generated preparation/preset tables in a temporary host C
fixture. All passed. All **44 abilities** passed the configured preprocessor
table with species aliases resolved. Point spreads parse legally and preserve
the 66-point budget. These are actual-function access/configuration checks,
**not native battles for these eleven encounters**. No claim that the AI chose
each intended line has been made.

Important corrected legality: Grimer Knock Off is legal in the direct native
preparation array, including its reviewed extension. Replacing it with Drain
Punch is a coverage/recovery choice, not a legality repair. Slowpoke Scald is
also legal in the direct native array; keeping Hydro Pump here is a power/risk
choice. The removed master audit's narrower imported learnset was not the game
rule. No Item Clause is assumed for fixed campaign teams.

Available counterplay examples are grounded in current `src/data/wild_encounters.json`:
Route101 has Mienfoo, Taillow, Pawmi and Poochyena; Route102 has Timburr,
Pachirisu, Ralts, Marill and Natu; Route103 has several Electric choices and
Growlithe; Petalburg Woods has Shroomish and other Grass/Bug options. These are
alternatives, not mandatory starter/capture prescriptions. Free preparation
provides legal move/item customization. Later Route115/116 additions widen the
pool, but an encounter is not assumed to require access to grass behind a later
field gate.

## E0010 — TRAINER_GRUNT_PETALBURG_WOODS, Petalburg Woods

**Intention: 8/10.** A recognizably Aqua opening: fragile fast offense beside
bulky Water pressure, then a utility-backed reserve phase.

Carvanha changes Life Orb to Focus Sash, keeping Adamant Attack/Speed and
Protect. Protect can earn Speed Boost when needed; immediate attacks remain
valid. Its Normal unboosted Speed is 56, becoming 84 after one Speed Boost.
Wailmer retains Modest HP/SpA and Sitrus: Water Spout rewards health, while new
Scald replaces Ice Beam so chip does not remove its reliable Water attack.
Icy Wind's 23-Speed user is not pretending to be a fast setter.

Grimer retains physical bulk, Poison Touch and Black Sludge, but Drain Punch
now covers Rock/Steel and restores HP. Poochyena changes from Attack investment
and Crunch to Timid HP/Speed with Foul Play, Snarl and Helping Hand. Foul Play
uses the target's Attack, so its own 32 Attack points were not needed for this
role. Speed 53 allows useful Snarl timing; priority Helping Hand supports either
surviving attacker. Species are retained for the first Aqua identity rather
than adding another unrelated early sweeper.

Counterplay: Mienfoo/Timburr Fighting pressure challenges the Dark members;
Electric/Grass pressures the Water lead. Fake Out, multiple hits or two attacks
can defeat Sash; chip weakens Spout but not Scald. Better than 8 would require a
more distinctive transition between the Water lead and Grimer/Poochyena than
general damage mitigation—without adding a second forced setup phase to this
story fight. No individual battle runtime evidence yet.

## E0011 — TRAINER_JAMES_1, Petalburg Woods

Historical source-only review below, superseded by the actual-party AI review
in ai_restart_0011.md: Eviolite Dewpider, bulky Venipede, RAIN|PRESSURE and
native-tested offensive-drop/recoil/berry/contact repairs. Current intention
rating8.5/10; do not use this older paragraph as current loadout or evidence.

**Intention: 8/10.** Rain insects with an immediate physical beneficiary.

Surskit and Dewpider now lead together; Venipede moves to reserve. Surskit
replaces Sticky Web with Struggle Bug: it can establish rain, attack, slow both
foes, or lower special pressure instead of relying on a hazard before reserves
are relevant. Timid SpA/Speed and Sash retain an attacking setter. At Normal,
Surskit 61 precedes Dewpider 15, so rain can strengthen Water Bubble Liquidation
that turn. Swift Swim doubles **Surskit's** Speed; it does not accelerate
Dewpider. Dewpider's Adamant HP/Attack, Sitrus and drain/protection options
deliberately buy durability for its slow attack.

Venipede keeps Speed Boost, Eviolite and physical Speed investment; Toxic Spikes
is conditional on meaningful opposing reserves, not a first-turn instruction.
Kricketune keeps Life Orb/Technician and accurate Bug Bite/Aerial Ace with Taunt.
This retains one purposeful Venipede while Jose's later copy becomes Venonat;
Kricketune repeats as a regional insect but not as another rain setter.

Counterplay: physical focus before Dewpider acts, deny/replace rain, or exploit
Water immunity. Flying/Rock still pressure the roster. Fire is not a universal
answer to a Water Bubble/rain Water pair. A richer late rain interaction than
the two independent physical reserves could raise this above 8, but replacing
them with more Water insects would worsen regional repetition. No James battle
has been exercised; native rain mechanics are not proof of its actual choices.

## E0012 — TRAINER_WINSTON_1, Route104

Historical source-only review below, superseded by `ai_restart_0012.md` for
current items, native stats, executable repairs and bounded evidence. Its old
Sash description and85 SpA claim are not current runtime facts.

**Intention: 8/10.** A slow collector team with a genuinely dangerous Wooloo ace.

Quiet Porygon replaces Ice Beam with Trick Room; Eviolite, Download and 32
HP/32 SpA preserve both setter bulk and immediate offense. Porygon's native
SpA is 85, not the broken metadata reader's former value of 8. Normal Speeds are
Porygon16, Honedge13, Gimmighoul9 and Wooloo18, so Room has a coherent party-wide
purpose against fast invested foes. Honedge retains Brave HP/Attack, Life Orb,
Swords Dance and Shadow Sneak; setup is conditional, while priority still acts
by its own bracket inside Room.

Gimmighoul retains its special Ghost role, Sash and Substitute as a positioning
choice, not a guaranteed safe action. Wooloo changes defensive points into
HP/Attack and Brave Nature: its Normal Attack becomes 55. Cotton Guard plus
Fluffy supplies physical resilience while Double-Edge actually threatens a KO.
Leftovers offsets some chip/recoil. Thunder Wave is an alternative outside
Room, not an instruction to help foes inside it. The four unusual collector
species are retained; removing Hector's second early Porygon gives this one a
clearer regional identity.

Counterplay: Taunt/pressure Porygon, use your own slower attacker or consume
Room turns. Fire/Ghost/Ground challenges Honedge; non-contact Fire is especially
valuable against Fluffy. Better than 8 would connect the last-slot Wooloo phase
more deliberately to a surviving setter, since Room can expire before the ace
arrives. That is a phase-design issue, not an assertion that every battle must
keep Room up. No individual runtime result.

## E0013 — TRAINER_HALEY_1, Route104

Historical source-only review below. `ai_restart_0013.md` supersedes it for
current Fire Punch coverage, executable Wish/Trace repairs and native evidence.

**Intention: 8/10.** Immediate partner support instead of two competing opening
plans.

Ralts replaces Calm Mind with Helping Hand and the unused SETUP classification
is removed. Timid SpA/Speed and Sash still permit real Psychic/Fairy damage;
Normal Speed55 precedes bulky Eevee21 for Thunder Wave, while Helping Hand's
priority supports an attack irrespective of that ordinary order. Eevee keeps
Eviolite, Adaptability, Adamant HP/Attack and Double-Edge/Quick Attack. Wish heals
Eevee's position or a replacement there, **not its currently active partner**.

Marill retains Huge Power, HP/Attack and Sitrus, with Aqua Jet and Brick Break
offering a different damage type and screen removal. Snubbull remains the
reserve Intimidate window with Lum, Fire Fang and Fairy damage. Ralts can
support either if it remains active. These familiar early species are kept
because their roles are readable and accessible to the player, rather than
replaced solely to increase a distinct-species count.

Counterplay: two hits or Taunt limit Ralts; Ghost denies Double-Edge, while
Steel/Poison contests Fairy pressure without ignoring Fire Fang/Brick Break.
Available Fighting and special attackers can pressure different slots. A more
distinct reserve interaction than overlapping Marill/Snubbull Fairy offense
would raise the design beyond 8, but both currently provide nonidentical entry
and coverage tools. No individual runtime result.

**Intention: 8/10.** Two working pairs rather than a last-two Baton Pass promise.

Plusle/Minun keep Plus/Minus, Timid SpA/Speed and Sash/Sitrus. Both have configured

## E0015 — TRAINER_IVAN, Route104

Historical source-review snapshot below. Superseded for executable-AI completion
by `ai_restart_0015.md`: native item-loss forecast repairs, accepted pressure
preference, 28 actual-party scenarios and 7.5/10 intention rating.

**Intention: 8.5/10.** A real Shell Smash speed threshold with partner-safe damage.

Tentacool moves 32 SpDef points into Speed, keeping 32 HP, Bold and Eviolite.
Clamperl moves its 30 surplus HP points into the fast-special spread, keeping
32 SpA and Deep Sea Tooth. At Normal the order is Tentacool57, Clamperl48;
after Smash Clamperl reaches96. The previous zero-Speed Clamperl went16→32,
still behind many unboosted invested early attackers. Tentacool can Acid Spray
before an unboosted Clamperl attack, but after Smash the new faster Clamperl

Chewtle retains physical Speed investment, Life Orb and Strong Jaw coverage,
changing the attack category. Chinchou retains bulky special offense, Sitrus
and Volt Absorb as the meaningful Electric-entry option. Muddy Water is
partner-safe; no Surf immunity is being invented. All Water species fit Ivan's
identity; the unusually powerful Clamperl interaction distinguishes this from
James's rain insects.

Counterplay: punish Clamperl's lower HP/defensive drops, deny Smash or focus it
before its advantage develops. Grass remains useful; Chinchou prevents a
single Electric move from solving the entire team. A stronger reserve-led
second phase rather than general coverage could lift this above8.5 while
preserving Fisher identity. Engine limitation: the fast pair forecast currently
does not apply Acid Spray's immediate SpDef drop to the partner's cached damage;
the native move works, but its payoff can be undervalued. No Ivan runtime result.

## E0017 — TRAINER_CYNDY_1, Route115

Historical source-review snapshot below. Executable-AI completion and current
team supersede it in `ai_restart_0017.md`: Feint forecast repair, Psycho Cut,
Eviolite Clobbopus and pressure preference; 27 native parameters, 8.5/10 rating.

**Intention: 8/10.** Coaching creates physical pressure; the reserve is more than
another generic Fighting attack.

Riolu/Meditite retain Jolly Attack/Speed, both reaching Normal Speed60. Prankster
Coaching has elevated priority, so it precedes ordinary attacks; Fake Out still
acts before it. Riolu's Sash and own damaging coverage preserve options when
support is unsafe or Taunted. Meditite's Pure Power/Life Orb makes the Attack
boost meaningful, with Fake Out buying a different sort of opening.

Crabrawler keeps Eviolite, Adamant HP/Attack and Iron Fist coverage. Clobbopus
keeps HP/Attack and Leftovers but replaces Brick Break with Technician
Power-Up Punch: the low-power attack gets its ability multiplier and can build
Attack. Feint breaks protection before a partner's ordinary attack; Taunt can
still interrupt negative-priority Room despite slow Clobbopus. These repeated
Fighting tools serve a dedicated class identity, not a mandate to reuse them
in unrelated nearby trainers.

Counterplay: available Fairy, Flying and Psychic options pressure this roster;
special attacks ignore Coaching's Defense boost. Fake Out, Taunt, spread or two
hits contest Riolu. A second mode against special pressure would improve this
beyond8, while the all-Fighting weakness remains intentional. Engine limitation:
Coaching is native EFFECT_STAT_CHANGE/TARGET_ALLY, but the current pair forecast
skips generic status changes and does not calculate its immediate Attack/Defense
benefit. No actual Cyndy choice sequence is claimed.

## E0018 — TRAINER_HECTOR, Route115

Historical source review below; superseded for current loadout, executable-AI
completion and rating by `ai_restart_0018.md` (native restart8.5/10).

**Intention: 8/10.** A poison-activation opening and an actual paralysis/flinch
reserve threshold.

Spinda now leads beside Zangoose. Zangoose trades Quick Attack for Protect,
preserving Jolly Attack/Speed, Toxic Orb, Facade, Close Combat and Knock Off.
Fake Out or Protect can buy activation, but immediate attacks remain available.
Normal Speed68 is useful without another speed field. Spinda keeps bulky
physical points, Sitrus and Encore; its Speed23 does not imply every Encore
will act first.

Seviper remains the special rival reserve with Modest SpA/Speed, Life Orb,
Infiltrator and Glare. Dunsparce replaces the second early Porygon. Serene Grace,
Body Slam/Rock Slide, Roost and Leftovers give a different late pattern. Its

at the time of this change were E0164 and E0412, well outside this opening.

Counterplay: deny Fake Out, mitigate physical damage or exploit Toxic Orb's
increasing cost. Fighting pressures the Normal members; Seviper alters that
matchup. Electric types resist paralysis; Ground alone does not block Glare or
Body Slam, and Ghost denies Body Slam damage. A more interactive Seviper pivot
than mostly independent coverage would lift this beyond8. No individual runtime
result; Serene Grace is chance, not a guaranteed lock.

## E0019 — TRAINER_MARLENE, Route115

Historical source review below; current loadout, executable-AI evidence and
rating are superseded by `ai_restart_0019.md` (native restart 8/10).

**Intention: 8/10.** Slow Psychic offense with support that acts in the correct
priority bracket.

Spoink remains the Eviolite/Thick Fat special-bulk setter. Slowpoke retains Quiet
HP/SpA, Sitrus, Regenerator, Hydro Pump and Yawn. Hydro Pump is retained for power
despite imperfect accuracy, **not because Scald is illegal**. Normal Speeds are

Baltoy replaces Ancient Power with Ice Beam for reliable coverage alongside

distinct from Winston's mixed collector/physical-ace Room team, though their

Counterplay: Dark/Ghost/Bug pressure, Taunt or focus on the single setter, slower
attackers, and consuming Room turns. Non-Psychic coverage matters against Dark
immunity. A carefully differentiated backup setter or an alternate reserve

## Remaining runtime/design limits

All eleven teams are individually inspected and edited, with explicit roles,
counterplay and investment decisions above. None has been labeled10/10. The
access/configuration checks establish that the written tools exist in the
current game, not that every choice is selected optimally. Current fast scoring
does model specific weather, Tailwind, Helping Hand and redirection effects;
generic Coaching/stat changes and Acid Spray's immediate defense change remain
forecast gaps. Long-range Wish/Baton Pass/recoil/resource planning is not a
complete multi-turn solver. Existing signature-battle native tests elsewhere
must not be counted as testing this batch.
