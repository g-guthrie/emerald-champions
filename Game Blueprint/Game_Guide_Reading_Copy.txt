1. THE EXPERIENCE
-----------------

Emerald Champions is a difficult competitive doubles adventure built on the native Emerald/Inclement/Expansion foundation. Battles should be memorable puzzles with strong Pokémon, useful support partners, competent AI and several lines of play. The player should improve at competitive decisions rather than spend hours fixing stats, acquiring ordinary equipment or repeating a grind.

The most important question after building or reviewing EVERY team is: “Could this team be cooler?” Ask it again after your own redesign. Judge the partnership, Pokémon choices, regional identity, alternate lines and counterplay. A legal team, a rare species or six filled slots is not enough. Keep a good low-stat or baby theme and adjust individual levels when strength is the problem. Do not evolve everything, replace unusual support with generic attackers or weaken correct AI just to make a fight easier.

Use a broad, coherent roster across generations. Preserve signature habitats and early useful choices. All supported Mega types should have meaningful opportunities, but a name on a checklist is not an executed showcase. Do not add battles or wild slots to satisfy quotas. Pokémon gifts, natural habitats, legendary scenes and opponent teams have different jobs; one does not substitute for the others.

Every retained campaign trainer uses doubles or a deliberately authored multi battle. Ordinary trainer teams generally have at least four members; every Gym trainer and leader has six. The player chooses two starters from one selected region and the rival uses the unchosen third. Ordinary wild and legendary captures remain singles. The opening Birch rescue is a bespoke low-level double before normal preparation.

2. RULES, PREPARATION AND PROGRESSION
-----------------------------------

The first Oldale visit, before the prepared Route103 rival, provides healing, storage, the Leveler, legal moves and build editing. Nature, ability, EVs, useful IVs, friendship and ordinary battle equipment are free preparation choices. Maximum PP is fixed at its fully improved value. A preset is a convenience, not a different legality system or permission to manufacture progression items. All ordinary battle berries are freely available for equipping and planting; they cannot become free manual medicine or resale profit.

Manual medicine and X-items cannot brute-force trainer or wild fights. Poké Balls remain usable for catches, and held effects and healing/revival moves remain ordinary battle mechanics. Battle-start held items, including consumed berries, are restored at settlement. Field Potions, Revives, status cures and PP recovery remain exploration conveniences. Centers, the refillable Vial and established rest points reduce the need to purchase them.

Easy, Medium and Hard adjust enemy levels only, by −4/−2/0 relative to the existing authored offsets. This September15 global calibration lowers every difficulty mode by two levels; the mode names and player caps remain unchanged. Earlier individual level seeds in review records predate this global reduction. Medium is the new-game default. They do not change teams, AI, story or the player cap. Each trainer member uses the live player cap plus its authored individual offset and the difficulty adjustment. Optional return fights scale with the live cap; old absolute levels in generated trainer records are previews. Opponents can exceed100 within the native1–255 representation. No hidden stat multiplier replaces level tuning.

Player caps rise after milestones, not during their boss fights. Captures, gifts, evolution and preparation respect the current individual cap. Legendary/mythical species above600 configured BST use floor(chapter cap ×600/BST), with a minimum of1. Species at or below600 retain the chapter cap. Ultra Beasts/Paradox species are not automatically restricted. Temporary Mega/Primal forms do not incur a second reduction. No additional Mega-ready penalty is approved.
