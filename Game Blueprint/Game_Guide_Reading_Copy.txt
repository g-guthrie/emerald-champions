EMERALD CHAMPIONS — GAME GUIDE, V4
================================

This book explains the game we are making. The hand-authored guide owns the intended experience, story and design rules. The generated reference below it carries exact teams, world data, the Mega register, the acceptance ladder and source dialogue; source files own those records and scripts/sync_game_book.py rebuilds and checks them. 0. THE GAME IN ONE SENTENCE
---------------------------

Emerald Champions gives the player Pokémon Champions-style freedom to build any legal team, then runs them through the hardest, most interesting competitive doubles campaign ever built: every fight authored like a top VGC team, every Mega represented on a trainer, six-member Gym rooms, and no grind between fights.

Everything that is not a battle does one of three jobs: deliver species and Megas to the player, tell the story between fights, or get the player to the next fight without friction. Anything that does none of those is cut.

Within those constraints the game must feel first-party, as if Nintendo shipped it. Every system that stays needs a real incentive: berries pay in Mega Stones and Celebi, soot in a Mega and Marshadow, Shoal in Glalitite then a charted ice-room legendary and decorations, the Circuit in Poipole, Genesect and trophies, secret bases and decorations in expression. A system with a token reward is given a real one or removed. Dialogue, directions, menus and rewards read as native and never assume an outside guide.

Protected, never reduced: the 341-encounter roster, doubles everywhere, four-to-six member parties, six-member Gym personnel, all 99 Mega Stones on trainers, two-Mega League bosses, shared AI depth, levels-only difficulty, and free build editing.

1. THE EXPERIENCE
-----------------

Battles are memorable puzzles with strong Pokémon, useful support partners, competent AI and several lines of play. The player improves at competitive decisions rather than fixing stats, buying ordinary equipment or repeating a grind.

The most important question after building or reviewing EVERY team is: "Could this team be cooler?" Ask it again after your own redesign. Judge the partnership, species choices, regional identity, alternate lines and counterplay. A legal team, a rare species or six filled slots is not enough. Keep a good low-stat or baby theme and adjust individual levels when strength is the problem. Do not evolve everything, replace unusual support with generic attackers or weaken correct AI to make a fight easier. Every trainer review answers Stats (role fit of nature, EVs, IVs, item, ability and partner order), then Could it be cooler?, then Overrepresented? against nearby and campaign-wide species and strategies, then Counterplay.

Use a broad, coherent roster across generations. Preserve signature habitats and early useful choices. Pokémon gifts, natural habitats, legendary scenes and opponent teams have different jobs; one never substitutes for another. Do not add battles or wild slots to satisfy quotas.

Every retained campaign trainer uses doubles or a deliberately authored multi battle. Ordinary trainer teams have at least four members; every Gym trainer and leader has six. The player chooses two starters from one selected region and the rival uses the unchosen third. Ordinary wild and legendary captures remain singles. The opening Birch rescue is a bespoke low-level double before normal preparation.

MEGA EVOLUTION
All 99 item-based Mega Stones are held by at least one trainer, and the generated MEGA REGISTER names each stone's signature owner (the highest-tier holder whose fight is the showcase), its secondary holders, its single world source and when the player can obtain the matching family. Special forms (Mega Rayquaza through Dragon Ascent, female Mega Meowstic, original-color Mega Magearna, the Tatsugiri variants) appear there as rows with their owner status; a name on a list is not an executed showcase until the ladder says so.

Ordinary trainers and the player have one Mega Evolution per battle. Sidney, Phoebe, Glacia, Drake, Champion Wallace and the optional former-Champion Steven exhibition may execute two. The budget belongs to the trainer, persists across switches and fainting, counts actual transformations and never resets for a new battler. No Gym leader gets the two-Mega exception. Mega Garchomp's ordinary stone lives with Caroline's Victory Road sand team; Cynthia holds Garchompite Z; Drake's second Mega is Dragonite.

THE LEAGUE
All five encounters are six-member doubles at incoming cap 96, each with two Megas announced before battle, and the five teams share no species. Exact sets live in the generated reference; these are the designs.
Sidney: Dark Aura pressure with protected fast finishers. Incineroar and Yveltal open, Light Clay Grimmsnarl screens, Urshifu breaks protection, Speed Boost Sharpedo earns speed and then trades it for Strong Jaw on Mega Evolution, and Mega Absol Z punishes direct status with Magic Bounce. Counterplay: Fairy pressure, burns, screen removal, Trick Room, priority, denying Sharpedo its window.
Phoebe: Ghost trapping, Encore and boost punishment. Mega Gengar traps while Mega Banette Encores an already-used move; Lunala carries Wide Guard, Marshadow steals boosts, Specs Spectrier and physical Giratina fill the lanes. Counterplay: Ghost escapes from Shadow Tag, Dark types against Prankster, Taunt, ability suppression, priority.
Glacia: Snow endurance with physical and special closers. Ninetales sets Aurora Veil, Mega Froslass can restart snow through actual Mega activation, Chien-Pao, Slush Rush Sandslash and Mega Baxcalibur convert the window, Specs Kyurem attacks specially. Counterplay: weather replacement, special Fire and Fighting, screen removal, speed control; Sword of Ruin also cuts allied Defense.
Drake: Mixed Dragon air force with a sun phase. Aerilate Mega Salamence and specially offensive Mega Dragonite form an airborne pair that makes Earthquake safe only beside each other; Koraidon brings sun for Reshiram and Dragonite's Flamethrower; Zekrom and Regidrago round out the ground. Counterplay: Fairy immunity, Ice, Wide Guard, weather or speed control, chip through Multiscale.
Wallace: Rain and mist with a durable special anchor and physical Mega finisher. Kyogre starts rain and Tapu Fini Misty Terrain; Mega Milotic (Water/Fairy, Prism Scales) anchors with Helping Hand and Recover; Huge Power Mega Starmie finishes; Ferrothorn and Palkia support. Counterplay: change rain or terrain, Wide Guard, target the correct lane, pressure Kyogre before Water Spout, punish a passive Recover.

The design audit order is major battles first: League, whole Gyms, Magma and Aqua story groups, then the remaining trainers. Rare species, Megas and major strategies are allocated around those core battles before nearby lower-tier encounters are refined.

2. RULES, PREPARATION AND PROGRESSION
-----------------------------------

STATS PLAY EXACTLY LIKE GEN 9 COMPETITIVE
A competitive player builds a Pokémon at the tutor exactly as on Smogon or in Champions. IVs default to 31 and are editable per stat from 0 to 31. EVs run 0 to 252 per stat, 510 total. Nature, ability, legal moves and friendship are free. All of this is available from the first Oldale visit, before the prepared Route103 rival, together with healing, storage, the Leveler and basic Balls. No level floor, EV ticket, IV lock or paid stat service may be added to soften the early game; every authored trainer is fully invested the same way, so early fights are symmetric, and difficulty is tuned by level offsets against fully invested reference parties. A preset is a convenience, not a different legality system. Maximum PP is fixed at its fully improved value. Technical and Hidden Machines do not exist in this game; the Center tutor is the only source of moves and teaches every legal one for free, and the Summary screen reports the tutor-set Nature and cycles the current IV and EV spread beside the level cap. All ordinary battle berries are free equipment and planting seeds; they cannot become manual medicine or resale profit.

HELD ITEMS AND HEALING
Manual medicine and X-items cannot brute-force trainer or wild fights. Poké Balls remain usable for catches; held effects and healing or revival moves remain ordinary battle mechanics. Battle-start held items, including consumed berries, are restored at settlement. Field Potions, Revives, status cures and PP recovery are exploration conveniences; Centers, the refillable Vial and established rest points reduce the need to buy them. Retry restores the current battle checkpoint and Reload restores the last saved game; they stay separate, and defeat costs no money.

DIFFICULTY AND LEVELS
Easy, Medium and Hard adjust enemy levels only, by −4/−2/0 relative to the authored individual offsets (src/difficulty.c). Medium is the designed game and the new-game default; Easy is a two-level handicap, not an easy game. The Options screen states those offsets under the Difficulty setting and also carries Text speed (Slow, Mid, Fast, Instant; Instant is the default). Modes never change teams, AI, story or the player cap. Each trainer member uses the live player cap plus its authored offset and the difficulty adjustment; optional return fights scale with the live cap and award no prize money. Opponents can exceed 100 within the native 1–255 range. No hidden stat multiplier replaces level tuning.

Player caps rise after milestones, never during their boss fights, and every milestone is a badge or an act climax. Captures, gifts, evolution and preparation respect the current cap. Legendary and mythical species above 600 configured BST use floor(cap × 600 / BST), minimum 1; species at or below 600 keep the cap; Ultra Beasts and Paradox species are not automatically restricted; temporary Mega and Primal forms incur no second reduction.

Caps: opening14; Roxanne20; Dewford manor/song24; Wattson30; Mt. Chimney36; Flannery42; Norman48; weather survey54; Winona60; Faraway return68; Heatran76; Space Center84; Juan90; Victory Road Wally96; Champion100. The entire League is fought at 96. Milestone dialogue reads these values from src/caps.c through the buffermilestonetext script macro; no script hardcodes a cap or stipend.

EVOLUTION
Evolution works locally, without another emulator or trade. Linking Cord is reusable; item evolutions use the item locally while keeping meaningful form, gender, location and party conditions. Free bonding and move preparation support friendship and known-move evolutions. The Leveler permits evolution confirmation at the cap; declining and Everstone remain valid. Repetitive walking, move-use, recoil and critical-hit chores are removed for their amended species and are never replaced by a currency, ticket or invented level floor. Finizen evolves at 38 and Gimmighoul at 45. Exact relationships are in the configured species tables.

FIELD ACCESS AND KEY ITEMS
Field access requires the relevant badge and license. Cut: Rustboro cutter, usable after Roxanne. Flash: Granite Cave hiker, usable with the Stone Badge before Brawly; lower floors light on entry without a moveslot. Rock Smash: Mauville provider, usable after Wattson. Strength: Rusturf reunion, usable after Flannery. Surf: Wally's family after Norman. Fly: Route119 rival, usable after Winona. Dive: Steven after the Space Center. Waterfall: final Sootopolis handoff, usable after Juan. Cut, Flash, Rock Smash, Strength, Surf, Dive and Waterfall are used by walking up to the obstacle; any party member whose species could learn the move performs it, fainted or not, and no field move ever occupies a battle moveslot. Dig, Sweet Scent and Teleport work the same way by species capability, with the Sealed Chamber wall asking for Dig on its own. Personal Fly is the Flight Beacon, a Key Item from the first Center, usable from the Feather Badge; research transport is separate. The Bag has seven pockets (Items, Medicine, Held Items, Berries, Poké Balls, Key Items, Mega Stones); a Key Item can be registered to SELECT, L or R with its button shown in the Bag, L is not offered under Button Mode L=A, and items with no field use cannot be registered. Mom gives the Old Rod with the Running Shoes.

Key items register to SELECT, L or R from the Bag's Register action, one item per button; the Bag list shows the bound button beside the item. The L slot is inactive while the L=A option is on. R without a bound item keeps its DexNav search. Bindings survive Save and Continue.

3. THE CAMPAIGN
---------------

Chapter numbers (C01–C50) are stable identifiers used by flags and scripts, not the play order. The play order is the four acts below. C06, C11, C15, C21 and C33 are stops inside their acts, never requirements to spend, breed, harvest, collect or win optional fights. Research is something characters do inside this story, not a second progression track: internal report flags record persistence, the player receives concrete dialogue and consequences, and generic turn-ins never become extra stages. Capturing a named legendary is optional unless an explicitly optional acquisition itself requires it.

Story gates enforce accomplishments, never suggested dialogue: museum after Dewford; Wattson after museum and Wally; Flannery after Chimney; Norman after the desert safeguards; Weather Institute after New Mauville; Winona after the survey and the Scope; expedition permissions after survey, Scope, Winona and the Lilycove rival; Maxie after the voyage; Heatran after Groudon; the submarine theft and Aqua Hideout after Heatran; the twins after the hideout; Space Center after the twins; Seafloor after the Space Center; Juan after the crisis; Diancie and Jirachi after Badge 8; League after Steven's conclusion and eight badges. Every required handoff below is a quoted direction that names the action, destination, entrance or person and the nearest usable resource. The same interaction repeats its current direction until its destination event completes; when it cannot remain, the existing departure interaction carries it. Any Center nurse retries earned travel documents and pending gifts; a full Bag never revokes an earned right, duplicates a payment or forces a boss to be fought again.

4. BATTLES, ACCESS AND RECOVERY
------------------------------

The current finite roster contains341 encounters across324 authoring groups and368 party variants. Gender and starter aliases and two-owner definitions are not extra played battles, and stable trainer IDs are not chronological numbering. Exact rosters, plans and counterplay are authored in data/emerald_champions/emerald_champions_battle_teams.txt (each group carries its strategy, Mega slots, plan and crack); the compiler projects them into native parties and AI configuration, and the generated reference prints every team. Per-trainer design status lives in the generated ACCEPTANCE LADDER (L0 authored, L1 AI fixtures, L2 native benchmark against the cap window's reference parties, L3 earned clear), not in prose here.

REVIEW METHOD
Audit in first-access windows: at every badge, license, transport and story unlock, reconcile the whole reachable roster against map objects, script-triggered battles, optional interiors and newly opened sections of earlier maps. Check physical approach plus story and license conditions; never sort only by E number or preview cap. Work in four-to-five battle blocks, whole Gyms and coherent story gauntlets. For each trainer: Stats, Could it be cooler?, Overrepresented? (nearby and campaign-wide species and strategy repetition, comparing actual partners, enabling move or ability, opening and fallback rather than broad tags), then Counterplay. Design review, source implementation and observed native execution are recorded separately; a reviewed team is not a calibrated one. Major battles come first: League, whole Gyms, Magma and Aqua groups, then the rest.

REFERENCE PARTIES AND CALIBRATION
Each cap window defines two or three of the strongest legal, fully invested player teams from its available pool; level seeds are tuned against those, because that is what a player with full freedom brings. Individual level offsets are the tuning tool; a low-stat theme may sit above the cap and an exceptional Mega below it. A first-attempt win with fewer than three new faints prompts a review (normally the lowest opponent), never an automatic increase; three failed optimized attempts prompt a review of preparation, mistakes and individual levels, never endless retries. Assisted party preparation is allowed for development with acquisition evidence and never counts as native catching acceptance.

SHARED AI
Every battle needs a strong plan, a worthwhile fallback and sensible partner preservation. The opponent never reads the player's pending commands: it decides at turn start from the current board, its knowledge flags, revealed sets, last used moves and native locks, and it forecasts what the player might do. Protect, Wide Guard, Quick Guard and Fake Out are scored on expected value under that uncertainty: a shield must bank real survival, a partner payoff (Trick Room, Tailwind, weather, a certain knockout) or a waiting payoff (recovery, a status clock, an expiring field), and it pays a tempo cost plus the native consecutive-use odds. There is no Protect quota or cooldown, and no set loses Protect to fix the AI. The complete decision budget is 1.2 seconds including setup and both actors. Demonstrated shared defects are fixed across move and switch paths with focused native regressions; owner indexes for shared mechanics (Choice locks, Dancer, resist Berries, Knock Off, damaging pivots, Rage Fist) are recorded in docs/VERIFICATION.md, not here.

RECOVERY AND ENDURANCE
Retry restores the current battle checkpoint; Reload restores the last saved game; they stay separate and defeat costs nothing. Ordinary adjacent fights keep individual clears and first-clear rewards; museum, summit and Space Center legs release control for recovery trips without replaying completed legs, and no unannounced free heal is added. Lilith and Brenden keep independent clears. Steven's team-up heal is an announced exception. The Winstrates are an announced optional four-fight endurance challenge with no between-leg healing or editing; failure or withdrawal restarts at Victor and never re-pays a permanent payout. The League allows field medicine, the Vial and portable preparation between rooms but no Center, PC, shop or refill; bring six, and ordinary defeat resets the sequence at the outside recovery point while Retry restarts the current leg. Permanent first-clear payouts and Champion completion are single entitlements.

CUT POLICY
The pacing direction was roughly 30% fewer trainer encounters, taken from repetitive lower-tier fights while preserving Gym personnel, important rivals, bosses, League fights and meaningful expedition battles. That program is complete: 148 identities are retired and absent from active teams, nonempty parties and Hoenn battle opcodes; their civilian actors and dialogue remain where authored. Gina and Mia (E0014, northern Route104) are an explicit keep. The roster stays open to justified changes, compared on pacing and distinct battle value rather than a count; no further blanket reduction applies. 5. REWARDS AND ECONOMY
---------------------

Money is small and narrow. It buys evolution items, Balls and cosmetics, nothing else, and every other reward is a Pokémon option: evolution items, Mega Stones, fossils, gifted Pokémon and encounter access. Ordinary held items, stat editing and move preparation are free and are never sold back as progression. Field treasure is not a money source: Nuggets, Pearls, Star Pieces, Stardust, Heart Scales and mushrooms sell for nothing, and every former treasure pickup holds a Ball or an evolution tool instead. Existing pickups, NPCs, shops and handoffs are the delivery budget; prefer replacing an unwanted item at a verified existing location over adding a sparkle, hidden item or actor, and never add collectible locations to fill a quota.

INCOME
Starting money is6000; milestone stipends are implemented in src/caps.c and total44000 including starting cash and Champion. Campaign prize money is the live cap times the trainer's prize multiplier (2 ordinary, 4 aces, Gyms and rivals, 10 leaders and admins, 16 Elite Four, 20 Champion) and is paid on the first clear only; return fights pay nothing, defeat costs nothing, and Pay Day pays only where prize money does. Campaign prizes total about 74,000 and lifetime income about 118,000 against a 46,000 paid catalogue, so the full catalogue is affordable around cap 68 and the Archive absorbs the surplus (docs/economy-ledger.md).

SHOPS AND PRICES
Evolution purchases cost500 per stone,1000 per ordinary tool and3000 per reusable device. Rustboro sells its six common stones plus Linking Cord from first arrival; Slateport sells ten stones plus the imported tools; Lilycove's department store carries the complete paid catalogue (1F reception and lottery, 2F everyday supplies, 3F catching and field recovery, 4F evolution stones and tools, 5F home and gifts, rooftop refreshments). Slateport keeps its catching specialist, field-goods stall, herbal stall, dolls, decorations and the Mart's imports; the decoration shop opens after the Route111 Secret Power camper's introduction or a Secret Base. Basic Balls are replenished freely up to10 at Centers, and the first nurse says so. Marts no longer stock Mail, Full Restore, Max Revive, Elixir, Max Ether, Repels, Escape Rope or Poké Doll: the free Repel Spray, the Vial and Center healing cover them. Current ordinary prices begin at100/300/600 for Poké/Great/Ultra Balls, with most specialist Balls600. Balls, free gear, keys and stones cannot produce resale loops. Mega Stones are never sold. Shops refuse a second reusable device or Mega Stone, scanning Bag, PC, party, boxes and Day Care. The generated reference lists every SKU, price rule, caller and stock condition.

DUPLICATES AND DELIVERY
An already-owned finite gift (a reusable device or a Mega Stone) pays a one-time cash alternative equal to the item's price (3000); nothing else is refunded, and no substitute can become a loop. Full Bag or PC storage leaves the delivery pending with the original receipt and never requires the fight again; any Center nurse retries pending papers. An NPC never promises a reward before checking eligibility, and a retired item never appears in a shop, prize menu, Pickup table or gift message (the release gate checks this).

MEGA STONES
No Mega Stone is discoverable before Steven grants the Mega Ring with Aerodactylite after Brawly; ground-stone actors and gift wrappers fall through until the Ring is carried. Each of the 99 stones has exactly one world source and at least one trainer holder; the MEGA REGISTER pairs each with the earliest family access. Roxanne's Old Amber, Devon's free revival and Steven's Aerodactylite are the first concrete pairing. A few later opponents may gift the stone they showcase after victory as one authored finite reward; never every Mega trainer. Trainer holding is separate from the player's source. Ground sparkles sit only on pickup tiles Inclement Emerald's designer authored, never two in one screen and never within three tiles of an item ball (scripts/check_stone_placement.py guards this); every stone that would have needed an invented tile is a scripted gift instead. The scripted gifts: Charizardite X from the empty cradle of the Mt. Chimney meteorite machine, Charizardite Y from the Winstrate mother after all four family battles, Emboarite from the woman in the Lavaridge hot spring after Flannery, Eelektrossite jammed in the New Mauville generator intake after the shutdown, Delphoxite as the Trick Master's final prize, Golurkite from the Fossil Maniac's brother, Flygonite from the elevated-ruins archaeologist, Latiasite and Latiosite together when the Southern Island encounter resolves, Mewtwonite Y from a Space Center scientist after the C42 report, Heatranite from the Magma Stone splitting when Heatran resolves, Tatsugirinite lodged in the stolen submarine's intake or from Captain Stern once it is recovered, Chandelurite and then Banettite from the Dewford Manor historian after the C14 report, Abomasite from a Weather Institute researcher after the C30 survey, and Froslassite from the Mt. Keep the habitat template unless a named family change is justified across the region: Magnemite's power station, Duskull's tower, useful opening babies, diverse underwater encounters.
